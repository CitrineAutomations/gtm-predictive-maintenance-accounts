# The three questions

Short answer first under each question, then the detail. We built this from public data with no access to the vendor's CRM, so treat every signal as a hypothesis to test.

## 1. How would you pick the 250?

**Short answer.** Rank parent companies, not CRM rows. Take parents with several plants in more than one state where at least one plant has a recent serious OSHA citation, and put machine-related citations first. Do not use revenue.

- **Roll up first.** We built a tree from plant records to the parent company (EPA Toxics Release Inventory). The 4,000 CRM rows would be mapped onto that tree, so a plant, a division and a parent stop competing as separate accounts.
- **Gates.** 3 or more plants in asset-heavy industries, plants in 2 or more states, and at least one serious, repeat or willful OSHA citation since late 2023. That left 373 parents.
- **Order.** Plants with lockout, machine guarding or mechanical integrity citations come first.
- **Check before outreach.** Is the start plant closed, sold or recently hit by a serious incident? If so, drop it, hold it or move to another plant.
- **Ignored: revenue alone.** The last list used it and got almost no replies.
- **Ignored: any EPA non-compliance.** The records we read behind that flag were paperwork and permit items. Narrower equipment violations are shown to the SDR as background.
- **Ignored: generic intent data and downtime statistics.** Neither points to a plant.
- **Not yet used: hiring.** A plant hiring maintenance or reliability staff looks like a strong signal in the one account we researched fully. It is the next one we would test.

With CRM access, the first change is to flag existing customers and reserve part of the 250 for parents that already have one live plant.

## 2. Who should the SDRs reach first, and does it change for existing customers?

**Short answer.** The plant manager and the maintenance manager at one plant. Yes, it changes for a customer: do not cold email, and go in through the plant that already uses the product.

- **New account.** Start at the plant with the signal. IT and procurement join once the plant team shows interest, because the deal needs all three.
- **The message** speaks about equipment between inspections. It never mentions a citation, a penalty or an incident.
- **Existing customer.** Tell the account owner first. Ask the champion at the live plant for results and an introduction to a sister plant, then lead with those results. Head office comes after there are two plants to talk about.

## 3. How would you know in 60 days if it is working?

**Short answer.** Closed deals will not show in 60 days, so run a split test on replies and meetings. If the signal does not beat the same email without it, drop the signal.

- **Two groups.** One email has an angle chosen because of the signal. The control email is the same without it. First and last lines are identical.
- **Fair assignment.** About one account in five is control, at random within company size groups, because large companies pick up more citations.
- **One result per account:** no reply, reply or meeting booked.
- **Also watch:** meetings with plant operations roles, and parents where a second plant gets involved.
- **Day 30.** Ask the SDRs which accounts feel wrong. Keep dropped accounts in the count so the comparison stays honest.
- **What it can tell you.** With 108 accounts in the demo (86 and 22), and even with 250, the test shows a direction. A small gap is noise.

## What we would want before trusting this

- Won and lost history, to see whether any of these signals appears more often in wins.
- Customer plants, so that expansion accounts are handled as such.
- Official OSHA data in place of the third-party copy we used.
